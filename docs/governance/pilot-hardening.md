# Pilot-hardening checklist

The prototype runs on synthetic data only. This page lists what must change before it handles real patients in a pilot: what is true today, with the evidence, what a pilot needs, who owns it and where it stands. It is a companion to the [pilot plan](../pilot-plan.md) (the G0 gate), the [assumptions and limitations table](../assumptions-limitations.md) (L-rows), the [regulatory mapping](regulatory-mapping.md), the [Malaffi adapter plan](malaffi-adapter-plan.md) and the decision log ([project/DECISIONS.md](../../project/DECISIONS.md)). Owners are roles, not people. Nothing here has been agreed with a provider or a regulator.

## The 100,000-case sweep against realistic volumes (estimate)

docs/evidence.md records no population of the emirate aged 40 to 75, so no figure is taken from it. As a labelled estimate, use the cost model's largest steady-state volume, 200,000 screened members a year (`tools/pilot/inputs.yaml`, assumed), and the FIT positivity of 0.07 (the pooled positivity in evidence.md section 1 is 7.28%): 14,000 positive cases a year. The number open at any time is at most the cases of the last year and, with the literature-anchored restricted mean time to closure of 167.9 days (`time_to_closure_rmst`, orchestrated arm in results.json, synthetic), roughly 14,000 x 167.9 / 365 = about 6,400 (cases that stay open for good would add to it). Even the full 14,000 is 7 times below 100,000, and the measured 10,000-case sweep takes 0.44 s with 4 workers (load.md).

A second, small anchor from the Abu Dhabi audit (Almansoori 2021, evidence.md section 8b): 45,147 eligible Emirati nationals in AHS clinics, 23.5% screened over two years (about 10,600) and 13.5% positive, which implies about 1,430 positives over two years (the paper reports 1,429), far below 100,000. This is an estimate on 2015-2016 data from one clinic network with mixed FIT and gFOBT, not a forecast.
