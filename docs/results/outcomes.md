# Health outcomes: cancers, deaths and QALYs from completed colonoscopies

Generated by `make evaluate-outcomes` (`python -m simulator.sim.outcomes`; seed 20261017, 20 replications x 2,000 cases x 360 days, 2,000 PSA draws, outcome parameters hash `f106b99cfc6b188c`). Machine-readable: [`outcomes.json`](outcomes.json). Byte-for-byte reproducible: `make evaluate-outcomes-check`.

**Read this first.** All patients are synthetic. The simulator's colonoscopy timing is calibrated, and its effect size comes from published navigation trials; the outcome figures then come from published observational studies, mostly outside the UAE. Nothing here was measured in Abu Dhabi, and no clinician has reviewed it. The numbers show the size of the health stake under stated assumptions. They are not a forecast of lives saved.

## Headline (literature-anchored scenario)

Per 1,000 FIT-positive cases, the orchestrated arm avoids about **2.3 advanced-stage cancers** (95% uncertainty interval 1.6 to 4.6) and **1.7 colorectal cancer deaths within 5 years** (1.1 to 2.8), worth **22 discounted QALYs** (14 to 38). At Abu Dhabi's central volume of 17,696 positives a year, that is about 42 advanced cancers (29 to 82), 31 deaths (20 to 50) and 383 QALYs (242 to 665) a year. Deaths avoided were above zero in 100% of PSA draws.

Central values use every parameter at its central value and the shares pooled over all replications; the intervals are the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of the PSA, which draws every parameter from its range and one simulation replication.

**Reading.** By language, deaths avoided per 1,000 range from 1.37 (en) to 1.95 (ar); after orchestration 32.8% of the ar group still has no colonoscopy within 360 days against 29.4% of the en group. By channel, deaths avoided per 1,000 range from 1.66 (letter) to 1.81 (phone); after orchestration 32.1% of the letter group still has no colonoscopy within 360 days against 31.2% of the phone group. By contact, deaths avoided per 1,000 range from 1.63 (valid) to 2.84 (wrong); after orchestration 55.9% of the wrong group still has no colonoscopy within 360 days against 29.4% of the valid group. By reach, deaths avoided per 1,000 range from 0.00 (out) to 3.16 (in); after orchestration 45.7% of the out group still has no colonoscopy within 360 days against 19.9% of the in group. Gains per 1,000 follow the completion gains, and a group that starts worse off has more to gain, so equal gains are not the test: the residual gap after orchestration is. What the system does about it: `make evaluate` reports the completion and closure gaps by language and channel (results.md, Fairness), and in the running service the fairness watch (api/app/agency/agents.py, `FairnessWatch`) raises a supervisor task when a language group's overdue rate, or a language or channel group's model-abstention rate, is more than 10 points above the overall rate. The watch checks overdue rates for Arabic and English only, not the 'other' language group, and does not look at contact quality; both are gaps worth closing. Cases outside the programme's reach gain nothing by construction (they are handled as today), so reach, not language, is the largest equity lever in this model.

## Limitations, in plain words

1. **Synthetic, not observed.** The colonoscopy timing comes from the simulator. Its 180-day completion ratio is calibrated to published navigation trials. Its timing between 180 and 360 days, which decides the delay groups here, is not validated (validation.md).
2. **The delay effect is observational.** Corley 2017 is a Kaiser Permanente cohort in California (2010 to 2014). People who wait longer may differ in other ways, so the odds ratios may overstate the causal harm of delay. The adjusted odds ratios are applied to the prompt group's risk.
3. **Non-completers are treated as completing after 12 months.** People who never have a colonoscopy and are diagnosed only when symptoms appear probably fare worse, so this choice tends to understate the gain.
4. **Survival is not from the UAE.** No UAE stage-specific survival was found. The central values are from a Saudi registry (2009 to 2017), and the upper ends are from US SEER. Five-year survival is used as a proxy for cure, so later deaths and late recurrences are ignored.
5. **Life-years are simple.** A death avoided gains the WHO 2023 UAE life expectancy at diagnosis, minus an assumed 2 years. There is no extra mortality for survivors, no quality-of-life loss during treatment, and no harm from colonoscopy itself (perforation or bleeding are not modelled). The first two tend to overstate QALYs, and the treatment-quality omission tends to understate them.
6. **Costs are illustrative.** The fee is an illustration, not an offer. The colonoscopy price is an aggregator range, and the treatment costs are first-year costs from one Saudi hospital. The extra colonoscopies are counted as within 360 days, and the colonoscopies non-completers would eventually have are not netted off.
7. **Equity groups are synthetic.** The language and channel mix is assumed. No nationality, age or sex variable exists in the simulator, so none is reported, and none is invented.
8. **No clinician has reviewed this model.** It needs review by a gastroenterologist and a health economist before any number is quoted outside this repository.
