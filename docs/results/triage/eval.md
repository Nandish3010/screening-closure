# Reply triage: evaluation (D13)

Generated by `tools/triage/evaluate.py`. Re-run `tools/triage/.venv/bin/python tools/triage/train.py` to regenerate.

**All data are synthetic**, written from templates by the developers (`tools/triage/templates.py`) and split BY TEMPLATE: the test set contains only templates the model never saw in training or validation. These numbers measure generalisation to new templates of the same kinds of message. They do not measure performance on real patients, whose language is wider, messier and different. See `docs/governance/reply-triage-card.md`.

## Setup

- Base model: `mlx-community/Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct-4bit` (Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, Apache-2.0, 4-bit), 0.88 GB on disk, run offline with MLX.
- Fine-tuning: LoRA on 16 layers, 250 iterations, batch 8, learning rate 5e-05, seed 20261018, loss on the label only. Training took 9.8 minutes on this machine.
- Adapter: 21.1 MB (`api/app/triage/adapters/`).
- Data: 2147 train, 371 validation, 551 test examples; nine intents; English, Arabic (MSA and Gulf), Arabizi and mixed script.
- Prompt: `Classify the patient reply intent: <text>`; the answer is one label. The confidence is the model's probability for its label among the nine labels (softmax over the first token of each label), divided by a temperature of **1.7036** fitted on the validation set.
- Mass gate: if the model puts less than 50% of its next-token probability on the nine label tokens, the reply goes to a person.

## Decision rule and abstain threshold

A reply is handled without a person only if its predicted intent is one of `confirm`, `reschedule`, `cannot_attend`, `wrong_number`, `dont_understand`, `already_done` **and** the calibrated confidence is at least **0.553**. `question`, `other` and `opt_out` always go to a person. The threshold is the smallest confidence at which at least 97.0% of the auto-handled validation replies are correct (target on test: 95.0%).

## Calibration (test set, after the temperature)

The temperature (1.7036) minimises the negative log-likelihood on the validation set (0.495 before, 0.412 after; on the test set 0.338 before, 0.330 after). Expected calibration error, 10 equal-width bins: validation 0.062 before and 0.033 after; test 0.029 before and 0.053 after. The temperature did not lower the test ECE: the model is already close to calibrated, and in the middle bins (0.6 to 0.9) it is now UNDER-confident. The threshold is chosen on the calibrated scale, so this makes it conservative, not unsafe; a different fit (for example on ECE) would move it.

## Classical baseline on the same split

Multinomial naive Bayes on character 1 to 4-grams, trained on the same training split, no abstention: accuracy 84.9%, macro F1 0.842, against 89.8% and 0.896 for the fine-tuned model. If these two were equal the small language model would add nothing on this synthetic data.

## How to read this

- Test and training texts come from the same hand-written families of message. High scores here are expected and say little about real patients; the H2 review of `h2_review_sample.csv` and a shadow period on real replies are the real test.
- The confidence is calibrated on synthetic validation data. On real data it will be less reliable, probably over-confident.
- The threshold was chosen on validation, not test; the test numbers above did not influence it.
