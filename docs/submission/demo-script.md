# Demo script: 7 minutes (6:30 spoken), Q&A and backup video

The live demo for the GITEX finals, if shortlisted, plus the answers to the questions we expect and the storyboard for the 3-minute submission video. **All data shown is synthetic**, and every number spoken comes from [`docs/results/results.md`](../results/results.md) (literature-anchored scenario) or [`docs/evidence.md`](../evidence.md); the source is given next to each line. The words to say, with a click cue every 15 seconds, are in [teleprompter.md](teleprompter.md) (spoken words only, under 900).

Timing rule: the script is written for 6:28 of talking inside a 7:00 slot (the teleprompter is paced at 2.3 words a second, 35 words per 15 seconds at most; its last block starts at 6:15 and runs about 13 seconds; re-timed for M7-4 and M7-3) and must be rehearsed to 6:30 or less (T-E11). Rehearsals and their timings are logged by the human owner (H3) in `docs/demo/rehearsals.md`; recording the backup and submission videos is H4.

## The shape: two beats

The demo has two beats, in this order, and each answers a different question.

1. **The catch (0:30 to 3:00).** Can a positive result go quiet? We make it go quiet on purpose, in front of the audience: the scheduler is killed, nothing is flagged, and then the watchdog and the escalation ladder catch everything, once each, in order. A person accepts the escalation.
2. **The reveal (3:00 to 4:30).** What does the system do on all the days nobody is watching? Behind the worklist an agency of agents prepares the work for every open case; the only thing in the inbox is a human decision; the clinician signs and the case closes only on evidence that exists and is complete; a patient's reply in Gulf Arabic is read live by a small offline model, and the rule is shown: a Gulf-dialect reply always goes to a person (the fairness audit found the model too often wrong on it), while the model can only flag, relay or stay quiet; and the patient's own screen is shown for about ten seconds, in Arabic, right to left.

Then the numbers (4:30, calibrated to published trials, with the Abu Dhabi sensitivity line and the labour figure, and the fact that the 40% target is **not met in realistic scenarios**; only the upper bound, a mechanism ceiling, comes close), the ask (5:45), one commercial line (6:00), which hands off to the Judge tour, and one health-outcome line (6:15): judges can replay all of this alone with the **Judge tour** button in the header (a 7-step, read-only overlay; `/judge` is the one-page summary). Governance has no section of its own: it is said where it happens (who may approve a message, who may close a case, what the amber banner means).

## How the live beats relate to `scenario.py`

`scenario.py` runs from the start of the story, in one go, and refuses to run unless the simulation clock is at its start and the case is at `acknowledged`. So nothing on stage can be a "scenario step". The only scripted part is run **before going on stage**: `make demo-scenario UNTIL=owner_accepted`, which takes the named patient's case through steps 1 to 3 (`result_received`, `auto_intake`, `owner_accepted`): the automation acknowledges and assigns it, the owner accepts, and the pre-approved result notice goes out. Everything after that is a UI action, and each step below names the scenario step it mirrors, so a rehearsal can use `make demo-reset && make demo-scenario UNTIL=<step>` to get to the same place. What the engine imposes:

- **The catch has to be staged in a particular order.** The sweep plans an obligation only for the state the case is in when it runs. So: the scheduler is killed, the patient is recorded as contacted (the state the SLA is measured in), the clock jumps, and nothing else touches the case until the scheduler is back.
- **The owner is Dr. Huda Rahman, not Dr. Layla Haddad.** The named patient is registered with Marina Digestive Health, so the automation named its default owner. In the web's **Role (demo)** switcher that login is **clinician**; **owner** is Dr. Layla Haddad (Riverside Primary Care) and may not act on this case.
- **The escalation ladder follows ADR-014.** An accepted rung stops the ladder, and only the role a rung is addressed to can accept it. Fifteen days is 11 working days: the supervisor rung (3 working days after the due date) is due and the coordinator rung (7) is not, so the supervisor accepts rung 2.
- **The whole worklist turns red.** The other 20 cases sit at "Acknowledged" with an owner who has not accepted, and their 1-working-day clock is long past after the clock jump. When the scheduler is back they are all overdue at level 3. That is the real catch and it is also why the agency has 22 cases to read.
- **Whole days, not exact hours.** The script moves the clock to exact hours and calls the sweep itself; live, the scheduler container sweeps on its own 10-second loop. Only the script's event log hash is reproducible (T-E9).
- **The closure is on a second case**, prepared earlier (E9): "Confirmatory completed", owned by Dr. Karim Nasser (role **specialist**), with the colonoscopy `Procedure` and its final report already in HAPI and a closure proposal in the log. It is in the snapshot, so `make demo-reset` brings it back.

## The 7 minutes

Roles are chosen with the header **Role (demo)** switcher; the clock moves are the supervisor's **Advance clock (days)** control. The sections add up to 6:28 of speech, which leaves 32 s of a 7:00 slot as margin for the clock waits and the Q&A, and the rehearsed run must come in at 6:30 or less. The role changes five times, once each, in this order: clinician (contact the patient), supervisor (clock, escalation), specialist (second case, inbox, closure), coordinator (the reply), patient (the patient screen); the agency run is started from a terminal, not from a supervisor tab, so it costs no switch. The watchdog raises the scheduler alert 60 to 80 seconds after the scheduler stops (measured 58 to 80 s, ADR-023), so the scheduler is stopped at 0:45 and the alert is collected at about 2:15, after the filler.

## Q&A preparation

**"Is this just a ticket tracker?"**
A ticket tracker records what people type. Here the rules are enforced by the engine: a case cannot close without evidence that resolves in the FHIR store, is complete and carries the closer's own signature as a physician (checked inside the state machine), and a property test checks that over thousands of random event sequences (T-A1). Clocks run on a working-day calendar and escalate in order to named people who must accept. Silence is detected: idle cases are flagged and a stopped scheduler raises an alert (T-A30, T-A31). Consent is an append-only ledger that freezes outreach in the engine. Every event is projected to FHIR R4 with Provenance. And the evaluation reports the escalation acted-on rate (within 5 working days), 30.5% in the literature-anchored scenario, so we can see whether escalations are acted on rather than assume it (results.md, `escalation_acted_on_rate`).

**"Why is there an amber banner? Who approved those messages?"**
Nobody has, and the banner says so. The shipped outreach policy is unsigned (`approved_by: null`) and an unsigned policy sends nothing. The demo sets `OUTREACH_DEMO_APPROVAL=1`, which treats the policy as signed by "DEMO PLACEHOLDER (clinical sign-off pending, H1)" and shows that text on the case page and the dashboard. A clinician has to put their name in the file before a pilot sends anything; the API refuses to start with the switch on outside development (A26, ADR-037). The AI-simulated clinician review in `docs/governance/` is not that approval.

**"What's real?"**
The engine, the tests, the FHIR projection and the evaluation code are real and run on this laptop. The data is synthetic: a Synthea cohort and a seeded simulator. Of 144 simulator parameters, 89 are assumed, 19 calibrated, 8 empirical, 13 sourced and 15 sourced-adjacent (`meta.parameter_status_counts`), and the assumptions table lists 37 limitations ([assumptions-limitations.md](../assumptions-limitations.md)). Put the table on screen, then: "run `make evaluate` yourself, and change any parameter you doubt." Nothing is integrated with Malaffi or a provider today. Notifications are mock-only (nothing reaches a phone), and the FHIR Appointment is a proposed stub, not real scheduling.

**"Why not use agents for this?"**
We do, for labour, never for authority. The agency prepares: it explains overdue cases, looks up evidence, assembles a closure pack, drafts a message for the owner to approve. The transitions that matter are safety-critical: is this case overdue, who is told next, may it close. They must give the same answer every time, be replayable from the log and be explainable to a regulator line by line. A model that decides a closure can be wrong in a way that looks right, and the case then leaves every worklist and every timer. Here that is impossible by construction: an automated actor cannot emit `close`, an agency actor can emit no event at all, and a model actor can emit only `message_drafted` (T-D2, T-A60, T-A61). If the model is down, nothing changes except that the draft comes from a template ([design-choices.md](../design-choices.md), [agency.md](../governance/agency.md)).

**"Can a bad colonoscopy close a case?"**
No, since A24. Closure evidence must be a final report that states a complete colonoscopy (or a CT colonography), or a completed Procedure linked to one. A Procedure alone, a stopped one, a report that is not final, one that says incomplete, or one that says nothing, is refused with a 422; the automation raises a review flag for a person instead of proposing a closure. About 11% of colonoscopies in the Abu Dhabi cohort were incomplete (Almansoori 2021). Still open: bowel preparation, histology and the loop back to booking (HZ-30).

**"Can a referral bounce around until nobody is counting?"**
Not any more, since A25. The 31-working-day colonoscopy clock starts at the first referral request and never restarts, so a case passed between providers cannot be handed a fresh 31 days (the clinician review's gap 2). The days before a provider accepts count against it.

**"Is the triage real? What if the model is wrong or down?"**
The classifier is real and runs offline on the host (Qwen2.5-1.5B 4-bit with a small LoRA adapter, trained on synthetic replies only), served by `make triage-serve` on port 8765; the API calls it with a 2-second timeout. On held-out synthetic templates, 96.2% of the replies it handles alone are right (target 95%), 58.1% are handled and the rest go to a person; every true opt-out went to a person (0 of 61 missed). Arabizi (81.0%) and Gulf dialect (91.1%) were below the precision target in the audit before the dialect rule (so the demo shows a Gulf reply going to a person, not being handled); with the rule that sends Gulf-dialect replies to a person and Arabizi only above a higher confidence, auto-handled precision is 100% in every group on the test split, at the cost of handling 43.6% of replies instead of 58.1% ([fairness.md](../results/fairness.md), "Dialect rule"; [eval.md](../results/triage/eval.md), [reply-triage-card.md](../governance/reply-triage-card.md)). Before any model, a deterministic keyword gate (English, Arabic, Arabizi) catches opt-outs, and an opt-out always goes to a person. If the server is down, slow or answers something malformed, the reply is read as needing a person: it fails safe. The Linux container has no MLX, which is why the model runs on the host. None of it is evidence about real patients (HZ-25).

**"Does it close cases faster?" / "How much sooner?"**
We make no claim about days saved, and the spoken demo says nothing about speed. B21 fixed the timing structure of the simulator and the held-out validation was re-run (validation.md, "Which timing claims may be quoted"). It now passes for two statements only, and we may quote them with the validation row: about 12% of positive results have a colonoscopy within 30 days under today's unmanaged process (simulated 11.7% against 14.7% observed in Australia; provisional), and the median time to colonoscopy among completers in the managed arm is about 6 weeks (42 days simulated against 37 at Kaiser). Everything else on speed stays withdrawn, including any figure for days sooner, because the baseline median fails validation (48 days simulated against 184 observed in a US safety-net system), so the difference between the arms cannot be quoted. What the validation shows beyond that is the baseline completion level against Mohl 2023 and the Azulay control arm; the completion ratio (about 1.31 at 180 days) is calibrated to published navigation trials, so it is not a validation result, and the held-out managed-programme levels (Kaiser 2017, Scotland, England) are under-predicted by 13 to 16 points. The pilot measures time to ownership and time to closure for real.

**"The labour target was 40%."**
Not met in realistic scenarios, and we say so: 36% per case the system handles (95% CI 34.9 to 36.9) and 20% across the programme (19.1 to 20.2), literature-anchored. Only the upper range, a mechanism ceiling and not a forecast, comes close to the line without reaching it, at 40.0% (a hair under; 95% CI 39.0 to 40.9, so that interval straddles it). Every minute is a published figure from an adjacent setting or an assumption, none measured in Abu Dhabi; coordinator time falls, owner-clinician and specialist time rise, and at wage-weighted cost the saving disappears (pilot-plan.md, K9). The pilot measures it.

**"What about capacity?"**
It is modelled (B19, results.md, "Under finite capacity"): a shared booking calendar for colonoscopy slots at three providers and a daily staff-minutes budget for the supervisor and the coordinator. At base capacity the 180-day gain survives: +15.7 points (66.6% against 50.9%), against +15.8 points with no limit. The 90-day gain is about zero (+0.7 points), and the median time to colonoscopy among completers is 15.0 days longer in the orchestrated arm (80.9 against 65.9), because more people are now in the queue. At 70% of base slot supply the 180-day gain is +5.2 points. So: more people complete, but the queue is endoscopy supply, and that is a buyer decision (slots, lists, referral pathways), not something the engine can create. Base capacity is itself sourced from NHS list sizes and assumed staffing, not measured in Abu Dhabi, per-endoscopist rosters and urgent-patient priority are not modelled, and the pilot measures coordinator minutes per case and, on a reviewed sample, the share of escalations the coordinator judges necessary (K7).

**"Your 1.31 is just the relative risk you put in."**
Largely, yes, and we say so. The 180-day completion ratio is calibrated: `orchestration.programme_reach` (0.55) was fitted in B12 so that the ratio matches the pooled navigation RR of 1.27. It is not independent evidence that the system works. What we show is the mechanism and what drives it (reach, owners, clocks, escalations), and the guarantees the engine gives by construction (no closure without evidence and a signature, no silent loss among the cases it reaches). Only the Azulay comparison is independent of the calibration; the held-out managed-programme completion levels (Kaiser 2017, Scotland, England) are under-predicted by 13 to 16 points ([validation.md](../results/validation.md)). Effect on real patients is what the pilot measures.

**"You opened with 30.5%. What about Abu Dhabi?"**
The 51% baseline is a US-calibrated usual-care baseline, not Abu Dhabi. We ran a sensitivity with the baseline anchored at the 30.5% audit (Almansoori 2021, evidence.md section 8b, overlay `simulator/params.abudhabi.yaml`): baseline completion 30.6%, orchestrated 38.8% at 360 days (a gain of 8.2 points; the overlay treats the loss the audit does not explain as unrecoverable in both arms, so it understates the mechanism; results.md, "Sensitivity: an Abu Dhabi-shaped baseline"). That is simulated and a sensitivity, not a forecast, and it depends on the same calibrated reach. The audit is one population and one definition of "completed"; the pilot's shadow run gives the real baseline.

**"Who pays?"**
We think the buyer is a screening programme, and we sell a programme licence across pathways, not a per-site tool: FIT positives first, then cervical screening results, which already run on the same engine as a configuration pack (owner, clock, evidence, signed closure; not clinically reviewed and not simulated), then blood-based screening positives and breast recall, which are designed only. The target buyer is Abu Dhabi's public health programme (ADPHC); that is a hypothesis, not a relationship, and nobody there has agreed to anything. Avoided treatment cost alone does not cover the fee: on the literature-anchored rows it is AED 78 to 177 per positive against an illustrative AED 90, with the 270-day shares not validated ([business-case.md](../commercial/business-case.md) section 4.3). Priced, the licence is an illustrative AED 2.0 million a year for a four-pathway scope (two implemented, two designed only) against an operator cost to serve of about AED 1.71 million (hosting at Azure UAE North list prices, everything else assumed; business-case.md section 9); avoided cost from FIT covers 69% of it and labour is a net cost in today's routing, so the buyer pays for safety and outcomes: about 4,400 positives a year no longer lost silently, at a net cost of about AED 25,500 per advanced cancer avoided (synthetic). The case a buyer buys on is capacity (growing screening without growing coordinators), safety and the audit trail against the IFHAS timelines, and the first step is a read-only 90-day shadow run at a provider ([go-to-market.md](../commercial/go-to-market.md)).

**"What is the risk score, and is it validated?"**
It is a small model that orders the worklist after the overdue cases; it never decides anything and a patient never sees it ([risk-score-card.md](../governance/risk-score-card.md)). It is trained on simulator output only, so it demonstrates an ordering pattern and is not a validated model. Say "ordering only, synthetic, calibrated within tolerance", and do not quote an AUC or any other performance figure on stage. Under the DoH Responsible AI Standard (clause 3.1.4.4.2, via regulatory-mapping.md), production data is not used to train models, so retraining in a pilot would need DoH approval and consented, de-identified data.

**"Malaffi is HL7 v2, not FHIR."**
Agreed, and our plan assumes it. The official sources we read list Malaffi's minimum dataset as HL7 v2 message types, and no official document confirms a FHIR interface (evidence.md section 8c). FHIR is our internal model. The adapter plan puts an HL7 v2 adapter at the boundary: ORU for results, ADT for demographics, SIU for appointments, the message control id as the idempotency key, and an automated actor that can never close a case. It is a target, not a claim, and the pilot does not depend on it ([malaffi-adapter-plan.md](../governance/malaffi-adapter-plan.md)).

**"ADHICS does not allow cloud for health data."**
The version we read, ADHICS v0.9 control CM 4.2, prohibits cloud for health information unless DoH authorises it; the current V2 is to verify. So our hosting order is: the provider's own approved environment first, a UAE cloud with written DoH authorisation second, nothing else (ADR-026). The stack is containers and Postgres with no cloud-managed service, so it runs unchanged in the provider's data centre. Backups, disaster recovery and remote support stay in the UAE. The model host follows the same order, and without one the template is used.

**"Why these ages and these SLAs?"**
Eligibility of 40 to 75 with an annual FIT is sourced from the UAE National Guideline and DoH IFHAS V10 (evidence.md section 8a). The demo cohort is generated for the same 40 to 75 range, so the cohort and the eligibility rule agree ([data-provenance.md](../data-provenance.md), L02). The referral is to be requested within 15 working days of the result's release (IFHAS V10), and the colonoscopy completed within 31 working days from referral (National Guideline Appendix 2, evidence.md section 8b); our 31-working-day clock starts at the first referral request and never restarts (A25). The other SLAs (acknowledge 2, accept 1, contact 3, patient acknowledges 5, book 5, review 5, close 3, re-route 2 working days; the four steps before the referral add up to 11, inside the 15) are design choices in a short YAML file; the clinician review (H1) will set them, and a change is a config edit and a rerun of `make evaluate`, not code.
