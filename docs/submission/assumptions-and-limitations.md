# Assumptions and limitations

Submission document for Challenge 05. Team Humans Decide. **Every case, patient and number in this project is synthetic.** The organisers asked for "assumptions and limitations"; this page collects them in one place from across the repository, each with the file it comes from.

It consolidates; it does not replace the detailed register. The full table of 37 numbered items (L01 to L37, with impact, how to remove each one and its status) stays in [../assumptions-limitations.md](../assumptions-limitations.md), and this page cites those ids where one applies. Where two sources differ, the more specific file (a results or governance document) wins and is the one cited.

How to read the "Where it bites" column: `results` (what the numbers can be used for), `clinical` (needs a clinical decision before real patients), `demo` (what a judge sees), `scale` (what the tests did not reach), `deployment` (what a pilot needs).

## 1. Data, calibration and what the results are

| # | Assumption or limitation | Where it bites | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | **All data is synthetic.** A Synthea cohort (Massachusetts, so US names, addresses and care patterns) plus a seeded behaviour simulator. No Arabic names, no UAE demographics, no real patient, clinician or facility. FIT results are generated by our own script, independent of clinical history. Nothing is validated on real people. | results, demo | [../data-provenance.md](../data-provenance.md); L01, L03 in [../assumptions-limitations.md](../assumptions-limitations.md) |
| 1.2 | **The simulator is mostly assumed.** 87 of 138 parameters have no public source and are placeholders; 16 were calibrated, 8 fitted to open data, 27 taken from a public document that measured the same or a related quantity (many from other countries). Every absolute number is conditional on them. A one-at-a-time sensitivity sweep bounds the effect but does not explore interactions. | results | [../assumptions-limitations.md](../assumptions-limitations.md) (parameter status; L04, L06); [../results/sensitivity.md](../results/sensitivity.md) |
| 1.3 | **Calibration source.** The baseline arm was tuned to reproduce the Mohl 2023 US completion curve, so that it matches is by construction, not a validation. The only Abu Dhabi follow-up figure found (2015-2016, FIT and gFOBT mixed) is lower. | results | L05 in [../assumptions-limitations.md](../assumptions-limitations.md); [../results/results.md](../results/results.md); [../results/calibration.md](../results/calibration.md) |
| 1.4 | **The headline ratio is calibrated, not validated.** `programme_reach` was fitted so the 180-day completion ratio sits at the pooled navigation risk ratio (1.27, 95% CI 1.10 to 1.46); the result is 1.31. It was deliberately not refitted after the timing change. Held-out managed-programme completion levels are under-predicted by 13 to 16 points. The model shows a mechanism, not new evidence of effect. | results | [../results/results.md](../results/results.md); [../results/validation.md](../results/validation.md); L09 |
| 1.5 | **Only some timing claims may be quoted.** Held-out validation passes for two (the unmanaged 30-day share, and the managed median days to colonoscopy). The others (managed 30- and 60-day shares, quartiles, days saved) failed and are withdrawn. | results | [../results/results.md](../results/results.md) ("Which timing figures may be quoted"); [../results/validation.md](../results/validation.md) |
| 1.6 | **Intervals show random-number noise only.** Bootstrap intervals over 20 replications of 2,000 cases do not include parameter or structural uncertainty. | results | L10; [../results/sensitivity.md](../results/sensitivity.md) |
| 1.7 | **The headline model has no capacity.** Escalations cost nothing and slots are unlimited. A separate finite-capacity model shows the gain shrinking when slot supply falls (at 70% of base slot supply the 180-day gain falls to +5.2 points), with slot supply and staffing themselves assumed. | results, deployment | [../results/results.md](../results/results.md) ("Under finite capacity"); L08 |
| 1.8 | **Health-outcome and money figures are assumption-based.** Advanced cancers avoided and cost avoided rest on observational odds ratios from another population and an unvalidated 270-day timing. | 1.9 | **Population shares are assumed.** Language shares, channel preferences and response differences by language are inputs, so the simulated fairness split cannot show that a real programme is fair. | results | L07; [../results/fairness.md](../results/fairness.md) |
| 1.10 | **FIT positivity (7 per cent) assumes a sensitive cut-off.** Programmes with stricter cut-offs report lower rates; the only Abu Dhabi figure has no stated cut-off. Case volume scales with it. | results, deployment | L15; [../evidence.md](../evidence.md) |

## 6. Scale: what was measured and what was not

All scale measurements are from one laptop-class machine (a local three-node kind cluster, one Postgres without high availability or TLS, no network latency). **No cloud environment was measured.**

| # | Statement | Where it bites | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6.1 | **API pod memory is not flat (BUG-77, P1-16).** Over a 4.00 h soak the three API pods grew by 43.85, 45.63 and 45.94 MiB/h (anonymous memory), ending well under a 768 MiB limit, but at that rate a pod would reach its limit in roughly a day. The scan-path transient was found and fixed; **the cause of the remaining growth is not found**. Ruled out: the glibc heap and 64 MiB arenas (the maps), `MALLOC_ARENA_MAX=2` (30 min A/B, climbed the same), Python-level retention on the request path (tracemalloc, 60 rounds), thread growth (11 to 14 threads, flat), worker recycling (fails at 768 MiB: OOM kills). A 15-minute single-container replay of the soak mix at 50 req/s with the real scheduler and the cluster's settings (BUG-77 round 2, `tools/load/leak_soak.py`) did **not** reproduce it: after the first two minutes of warm-up the anonymous memory grew 8.6 MiB/h (OIDC tokens) and 12.6 MiB/h (dev tokens; 4.8 MiB/h over the last ten minutes), a curve that bends over. **Workaround:** a gated restart of the API container only (`deploy/gcp/vm/sc-api-restart.timer`: every 15 minutes, restarts only when its memory is at or above 70% of its limit; `sc-api-restart-daily.timer` is a daily fallback at a lower threshold; each restart is logged with its reason); on Kubernetes, restart the API pods at least daily (45 MiB/h is about 1.1 GiB per day). Not verified on a VM. | scale | [../results/scale.md](../results/scale.md) ("What is still not met after M7-8"; "Memory growth: root cause"), [../governance/operations.md](../governance/operations.md) |
| 6.2 | **The 1,000,000-case daily catch-up burst does not fit one 10 s scheduler loop** (M7-7 measurement, not repeated in M7-8). It is bounded and drains in 9 loops (2.4 minutes), but events for the last cases are written minutes late. | scale | [../results/scale.md](../results/scale.md) |
| 6.3 | **Sweep cadence is met after M7-8:** in the 4 h soak the sweep started 1,440 of 1,440 nominal times (100.0%, mean gap 10.000 s). The earlier 77.6% was the M7-7 serial loop. Not fully met: the automation scan started 99.6% of its nominal times (a tick that overran was skipped, as designed). | scale | [../results/scale.md](../results/scale.md) (M7-8 cadence table; "What is still not met after M7-8") |
| 6.4 | **One scheduler instance at a time.** Leader election by a Postgres lease lets several run safely, but a dead leader leaves periodic work undone for about the lease time. The real kill-and-resume of the task-based scheduler on the cluster was not re-run after the last change; fencing is covered by tests. On the hosted demo there is one scheduler on one VM, with no failover. | scale, demo | [../results/scale.md](../results/scale.md); L31; [../governance/hosting-gcp.md](../governance/hosting-gcp.md) |
| 6.5 | **Overload is refused, not served.** The highest rate that passes with nothing shed is 175/s (250/s counting graceful shedding); above it the API answers 503 with a retry time, and shedding adds no capacity. One soak window in 48 had a p99 over 500 ms. | scale | [../results/scale.md](../results/scale.md) |
| 6.6 | **The metrics routes are bounded in memory, not in time**, and the reconciliation cycle at 1,000,000 cases is slow by design. | scale | [../results/scale.md](../results/scale.md) |
| 6.7 | **The 100,000-case sweep meets its 5 s target on a laptop** (L34); the pilot hardware is not measured and a shared machine moves it by 10 to 25%. | scale, deployment | L34; [../results/load.md](../results/load.md) |
