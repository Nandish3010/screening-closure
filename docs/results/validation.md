## How a verdict is reached

- **Tolerance.** A completion (or share) row is *within tolerance* when the simulated mean is within +-5 percentage points of the observed value. A time row (median, 25th or 75th percentile of days to colonoscopy among people who had one) is within tolerance when simulated/observed lies in [0.75, 1.25].
- **Target verdict.** *Within tolerance* only if every **held-out** row of the target's primary variant is within tolerance. Rows whose observed number is also a simulator parameter are marked *in-sample*; they are shown but not scored. One failing row fails the target, so the verdict is strict; the per-row tables say where.
- **Coverage check.** The simulator's 95% replication interval is the 2.5th to 97.5th percentile of the per-replication values. It contains only cohort sampling noise (2,000 cases per replication) and not parameter uncertainty or between-setting variation, so it is narrow and an observed study will often fall outside it even when the model is a fair description. The table therefore also shows whether the simulated mean lies inside the observed study's own 95% Wilson interval (completion rows only).
- **Setting variants.** The baseline arm is today's unmanaged follow-up. Where the observed setting is a managed programme, an adjusted variant is run with only the documented differences: no forgotten staff steps (`staff.forget_prob` 0), no rejected referral (`referral_rejection_rate` 0) and no unanswered referral (`handoff.no_response_prob` 0), because the programme issues the referral and owns follow-up. Both variants are shown. No other parameter is touched for any target. Positivity does not enter these comparisons (completion is conditional on a positive test).
- **Clock.** The simulator counts days from the positive result. Observed clocks differ and are noted per target.

## Reading

**Where the model is good.**

- *Baseline completion at the level of typical US usual care.* At 12 months the simulated baseline completes 54.1% against 55.6% in the San Francisco safety net (-1.6 points), and at 120 days 47.3% against 47.1% in the Israeli control arm (+0.3 points). Both are independent of the calibration, both are within tolerance, and the Israeli one is a different country, test and health system.
- *The size of the orchestrated effect.* The simulated ratio is inside the confidence interval of every trial tested: 1.32 against 1.49 (1.27 to 1.75) for SMS reminders at 120 days, 1.27 against 1.16 (1.02 to 1.31) for the Kaiser NW navigation trial at 180 days, and 1.23 against 1.31 (1.15 to 1.50) for the 2025 Annals trial (not independent). The simulated ratio sits below the point estimate of the reminder trial and the 2025 trial and above that of the Kaiser NW trial, so the 1.27 is in the middle of the published range rather than at an optimistic end. Only the 120-day reminder trial is a test the calibration could have failed (programme_reach was fitted at 180 days).

**Where the model is off.**

- *Early timing is badly wrong, in every setting.* Almost nobody in the simulator has a colonoscopy within 30 days: baseline 1.7% against 14.7% in Australia (secondary source), and, among completers, 2.0% within 28 days against 28.8% in Scotland. The orchestrated arm reaches 2.8% at 30 days and 38.7% at 60 days against Kaiser's 33.3% and 63.6%, and its median of 56 days is 1.5 times Kaiser's 37. The cause is structural: the booking lead (24-day median, calibrated to Mohl) plus the staff steps put a floor of several weeks under every case, whereas managed and integrated systems book within days. Any claim about days saved, a 30-day SLA, or the 'time to closure' headline inherits this.
- *Time to colonoscopy among completers is too short in the unmanaged setting.* The baseline median is 61 days (IQR 46 to 85) against 184 (140 to 232) in San Francisco, a ratio of 0.33, and 0.74 of Sea Mar's 83, which is the parameter `time_to_colonoscopy_median_days` itself. That parameter is a reference that the simulator compares against but does not read, so the 61-day median is an outcome of the staff delays and the booking lead calibrated to the Mohl completion curve. The model matches Mohl's share completing but not the speed at which completers arrive, which says the calibration used the completion curve only and the shape of the time distribution is unconstrained.
- *The level of completion does not transfer between settings with one parameter set.* Against Sea Mar the baseline is +11.3 points at 360 days (41.9% against 22.9% at 90 days), against Kaiser NW usual care -14.0 points at 180 days. These are the two sides of the Mohl average, as expected from the 17% to 72% organisational spread, but it means the model is a model of the average organisation and the gain it reports for any one clinic depends on where that clinic starts.
- *Managed national programmes are out of reach for the baseline.* Scotland and England attend 75.0% and 76.0%; the baseline with the managed adjustments reaches 61.6% (-13.4 and -14.3 points), and without them 54.1%. The remaining gap is probably the model's never-complete share (refusal, drop-out), which the three adjustments do not touch; this was not isolated by an extra run. Whether managed programmes really have fewer refusers or the simulator over-states them is not identifiable from these tables.
- *The orchestrated arm does not reach an integrated programme in the literature-anchored scenario.* It is -16.8 points from Kaiser at 360 days (in-sample row), by design: that scenario caps the effect at published navigation levels. The upper-range scenario is within tolerance at 90, 180 and 360 days (-4.7 points at 360) but those rows are in-sample, and it too misses 30 and 60 days.

**What this means for the headline.**

The headline is a *difference between two arms*: about 14 points more colonoscopies by 180 days (ratio 1.27, literature-anchored). The validation supports the ratio (inside three trial intervals, one of them a fair out-of-sample test) and the baseline level for typical US usual care (two independent targets within 2 points). It does not support absolute levels in other settings, a managed-programme baseline, or anything about speed: statements such as 'closes the loop N days sooner' or 'colonoscopy within 30 days' rest on a timing structure that is wrong at the short end in every comparison that reaches below 60 days (Scotland, Australia, Kaiser). The headline should therefore be quoted as a completion ratio at 180 days with its trial-based range, not as a number of days, and the claim that it applies to Abu Dhabi stays an assumption: no target here is from the UAE, and the one local anchor (30.5% ever had follow-up, 2015-2016) lies below even the simulated baseline.

**What to do next** (not done here, owner's decision): constrain the time-to-colonoscopy shape in the Mohl calibration (the 61-day median is a free outcome; the sourced 83 days is not enforced) and give managed settings a lower booking lead; this changes parameter hashes and therefore needs `make evaluate`, `make sensitivity` and the risk-score retrain, as in `docs/results/calibration.md`.

## Limits of this validation

- **Few, small, heterogeneous targets.** Four US/Israeli cohorts, one integrated programme, two national programmes and one secondary summary. Real completion at 12 months spans 17% to 72% across US organisations in Mohl alone; a single parameter set cannot sit on all of them, and the verdicts above partly measure that spread, not model error.
- **Trial intervals are wide.** An interval of about 1.0 to 1.3 or 1.3 to 1.7 cannot tell a true ratio of 1.2 from 1.4. Being inside is necessary, weak evidence.
- **Approximate counts.** Counts for Sea Mar (from rounded percentages), the Kaiser NW arms and the 2025 Annals trial are rounded from published percentages (see targets file); this moves a trial interval by a few hundredths.
- **Transcribed values.** Corley's figures are transcribed from `docs/evidence.md` (graded P there) and were not re-opened for this ticket.
- **Not a blind test.** The author of this validation had read the calibration documents; independence here means 'no parameter was set from these numbers', checked against `params.yaml` and `docs/results/calibration.md`, not 'unseen by the team'.
- **Replication intervals ignore parameter uncertainty.** `make sensitivity` covers that; this document does not.
