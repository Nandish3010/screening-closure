## How a verdict is reached

- **Tolerance.** A completion (or share) row is *within tolerance* when the simulated mean is within +-5 percentage points of the observed value. A time row (median, 25th or 75th percentile of days to colonoscopy among people who had one) is within tolerance when simulated/observed lies in [0.75, 1.25].
- **Target verdict.** *Within tolerance* only if every **held-out** row of the target's primary variant is within tolerance. Rows whose observed number is also a simulator parameter are marked *in-sample*; they are shown but not scored. One failing row fails the target, so the verdict is strict; the per-row tables say where.
- **Coverage check.** The simulator's 95% replication interval is the 2.5th to 97.5th percentile of the per-replication values. It contains only cohort sampling noise (2,000 cases per replication) and not parameter uncertainty or between-setting variation, so it is narrow and an observed study will often fall outside it even when the model is a fair description. The table therefore also shows whether the simulated mean lies inside the observed study's own 95% Wilson interval (completion rows only).
- **Setting variants.** The baseline arm is today's unmanaged follow-up. Where the observed setting is a managed programme, an adjusted variant is run with only the documented differences: no forgotten staff steps (`staff.forget_prob` 0), no rejected referral (`referral_rejection_rate` 0) and no unanswered referral (`handoff.no_response_prob` 0), because the programme issues the referral and owns follow-up. Both variants are shown. No other parameter is touched for any target. Positivity does not enter these comparisons (completion is conditional on a positive test).
- **Programme management (B21).** The managed variants also set `baseline.programme_managed` true, which raises the share of cases booked in the fast lane from `appointment.fast_lane_share.unmanaged` to `appointment.fast_lane_share.managed`. The orchestrated arm treats every case it reaches as managed.
- **Clock.** The simulator counts days from the positive result. Observed clocks differ and are noted per target.

## Calibration and validation split (B21)

The timing structure (ticket B21) is a two-lane mixture for the first booking: a **fast lane** in which the referral is made when the result is reviewed (no separate delay for ownership, contact and booking) and the wait from booking to procedure is the managed-programme wait, and a **slow lane** with the old staff steps and a heavy-tailed unmanaged wait. The share of cases in the fast lane depends on whether the pathway is programme-managed. Every parameter of the mixture is set from calibration data only; the held-out targets were not read while fitting (`simulator/sim/timing_fit.py` never opens `validation_targets.json`), and what the fitted values reproduce is in `docs/results/timing_fit.json`.

### Effect size of the orchestrated arm against trials

The simulated ratio is orchestrated completion over baseline completion at the trial's horizon, literature-anchored scenario (the one to quote), paired by replication seed. The trial ratio is the risk ratio of the two arms with a Katz 95% interval from the arm counts (counts that the paper does not publish are rounded from its percentages, noted below). Verdict: is the simulated ratio inside the trial's interval?

| Trial | Intervention | Horizon | Trial arms (treated vs control) | Trial risk ratio (95% CI) | Simulated ratio (replication interval) | Simulated completion (baseline, orchestrated) | Independence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Azulay 2019: SMS reminders after positive FOBT (Israel), 120 days | reminder | 120 d | 70.3% vs 47.1% | 1.49 (1.27 to 1.75) | 1.36 (1.33 to 1.39) | 47.1%, 64.2% | independent | **inside the trial's 95% CI** |

| Coronado 2025: navigation RCT in a 32-clinic FQHC network, 12 months | navigation | 360 d | 55.1% vs 42.0% | 1.31 (1.15 to 1.50) | 1.26 (1.23 to 1.28) | 54.5%, 68.4% | not independent | **inside the trial's 95% CI** |

- *Azulay 2019: SMS reminders after positive FOBT (Israel), 120 days* (independent): Not used for calibration. Our orchestrated arm is a bundle (ownership, reminders, escalation), the trial an SMS alone, so this is an upper-side test: the simulated ratio should not be far below the trial's.
- *Coronado 2021: precision navigation RCT (Kaiser NW), 180 days* (partial): The pooled RR 1.27 that fixes programme_reach (J Gen Intern Med 2026 meta-analysis) pooled navigation studies, and this trial may be among them (the abstract does not list them); the 15-day median reduction comes from it. Counts are rounded from the reported 75.5% and 64.9% (not published).
- *Coronado 2025: navigation RCT in a 32-clinic FQHC network, 12 months* (not independent): Cited in the source field of orchestration.programme_reach as the agreeing 12-month RCT, so it was read while calibrating the effect. Reported for completeness and NOT counted as independent. Counts are rounded from the adjusted 55.1% and 42.1%.

## Reading

**What B21 changed.** The timing structure, not a fit to these targets: a fast lane (booked when the result is reviewed, the managed-programme wait, calibrated to the Scottish 4- and 8-week bands) and a slow lane (the old staff steps and a heavier-tailed wait), mixed by programme management. Before B21 almost nobody had a colonoscopy within 30 days in any arm or setting; that floor is gone.

**Where the model is good.**

- *Early timing in an unmanaged national programme.* The baseline puts 11.7% of positives through colonoscopy within 30 days against 14.7% in Australia (-3.0 points; was 1.7%). This target is independent of the calibration, but its source is secondary (grade S), so the pass is provisional.
- *Speed of an actively managed pathway, middle and upper part.* The literature-anchored orchestrated arm's median is 42 days against Kaiser's 37 (ratio 1.14; was 56) and its 75th percentile 62 against 62. These Corley rows are held out (Corley is a partly independent target only because its 3, 6 and 12-month completion caps the orchestrated arm).
- *Baseline completion at the level of typical US usual care* is unchanged: 54.5% at 12 months against 55.6% in San Francisco and 47.1% at 120 days against 47.1% in the Israeli control arm, both independent and within tolerance.
- *The size of the orchestrated effect* is still inside every trial interval: 1.36 against 1.49 (1.27 to 1.75) for SMS reminders at 120 days (the independent test), 1.31 against 1.16 (1.02 to 1.31) for Kaiser NW at 180 days, at the upper edge of its interval, and 1.26 against 1.31 (1.15 to 1.50) for the 2025 trial (not independent).

**The reach decision (circularity).** `orchestration.programme_reach` (literature-anchored, 0.55) was fitted in B12 so that the 180-day ratio equalled the pooled navigation RR of 1.27 (J Gen Intern Med 2026). That meta-analysis pools navigation trials in health-disparity populations and very probably contains the 2025 FQHC trial and possibly the Kaiser NW trial, two of the three trials the ratio is scored against here. Refitting reach to 1.27 after the B21 change and then scoring the ratio against those trials would be circular, so **reach was kept at 0.55 and not refitted**; the ratio is reported where it lands (1.31 at 180 days in `results.md`, inside the pooled interval 1.10 to 1.46). Only the Azulay comparison is a test the reach anchor could not have seen.

**Where the model is still off.**

- *Early completion in the managed arm is still too slow at the short end.* The orchestrated arm reaches 17.7% by 30 days and 50.2% by 60 against Kaiser's 33.3% and 63.6%, and its 25th percentile is 29 days against 23. Part of this is reach (45% of cases follow today's process in this scenario: the upper-range variant reaches 66.6% at 60 days, within tolerance) and part is that the fast lane is the Scottish wait, slower than Kaiser's in-house endoscopy.
- *The unmanaged US completer distribution is now too fast, not too slow.* The baseline median is 48 days (IQR 32 to 81; was 61) against 184 (140 to 232) in San Francisco and Sea Mar's 83 (in-sample). The Mohl curve that sets the unmanaged mix has 77% of 12-month completers done by 90 days, so a model fitted to it cannot also have a 184-day median; San Francisco is a slower setting than the Mohl average, and the slow lane fitted to Mohl is only moderately heavy-tailed (sigma 0.84 against the fast lane's 0.64).
- *The level of completion does not transfer between settings with one parameter set* (unchanged): Sea Mar +11.8 points at 360 days, Kaiser NW usual care -14.1 at 180 days.
- *Managed national programmes still attend more than the simulated managed baseline:* 62.4% against 75.0% (Scotland) and 76.0% (England). Prompt booking raised it by under one point; the gap is the never-complete share (refusal, drop-out), which timing does not touch.
- *Scotland's timing rows are now in-sample* (the fast lane is fitted to them), so their closeness (66.9% within 8 weeks against 70.4%) is not evidence. They differ from the fit's own reproduction (`timing_fit.json`) because the fit measures from the referral (`accept_ownership`) and this comparison from the result.

**What this means for the headline.** The headline stays a completion ratio at 180 days (literature-anchored 1.31 after B21, inside the trial intervals and the pooled navigation interval). Two timing statements may now be quoted, each with its validation row: about 12% of positives have a colonoscopy within 30 days under today's unmanaged process (against 14.7% in Australia, provisional), and the median time to colonoscopy among completers in the managed arm is about 6 weeks (42 days against Kaiser's 37). Everything else about speed stays withdrawn: a 30- or 60-day share for the managed arm, US usual-care medians, and any 'N days sooner' figure (the baseline median fails against San Francisco, so a difference of medians has no validated baseline). The claim that any of this applies to Abu Dhabi remains an assumption: no target is from the UAE.

## Limits of this validation

- **Few, small, heterogeneous targets.** Four US/Israeli cohorts, one integrated programme, two national programmes and one secondary summary. Real completion at 12 months spans 17% to 72% across US organisations in Mohl alone; a single parameter set cannot sit on all of them, and the verdicts above partly measure that spread, not model error.
- **Trial intervals are wide.** An interval of about 1.0 to 1.3 or 1.3 to 1.7 cannot tell a true ratio of 1.2 from 1.4. Being inside is necessary, weak evidence.
- **Approximate counts.** Counts for Sea Mar (from rounded percentages), the Kaiser NW arms and the 2025 Annals trial are rounded from published percentages (see targets file); this moves a trial interval by a few hundredths.
- **Transcribed values.** Corley's figures are transcribed from `docs/evidence.md` (graded P there) and were not re-opened for this ticket.
- **Not a blind test.** The author of this validation had read the calibration documents; independence here means 'no parameter was set from these numbers', checked against `params.yaml` and `docs/results/calibration.md`, not 'unseen by the team'.
- **Replication intervals ignore parameter uncertainty.** `make sensitivity` covers that; this document does not.
