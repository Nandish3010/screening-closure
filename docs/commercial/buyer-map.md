# Buyer map: who runs and pays for screening in Abu Dhabi

Status: desk research, 25 October 2026 (ticket M7-4). **No organisation named here has been contacted, and no relationship of any kind is claimed or implied.** This is a map of the market from public sources, not a prospect list. Organisations are named by role only; no individual is named. Every fact carries its source. "To verify" means we could see it only in a search summary, the page did not load, or sources disagree. Many doh.gov.ae pages failed to load through our tools on this date (certificate errors), so DoH facts that were not already read first-hand for [evidence.md](../evidence.md) section 8 are marked to verify.

## 1. The shape of the market in one paragraph

The Department of Health (DoH) regulates and sets the screening standards. The Abu Dhabi Public Health Centre (ADPHC) runs the screening programme, IFHAS, for Thiqa cardholders, through more than 150 approved facilities in the public and private networks. Thiqa is government-funded cover for UAE nationals and is administered by Daman, so the payer of the downstream treatment cost is, in the end, the government. The facilities (SEHA under PureHealth, M42, and private groups) do the screening, the follow-up and the colonoscopy. Every licensed facility must connect to Malaffi, the emirate's health information exchange, and IFHAS participants are tagged so the programme is trackable through it. So the buyer with the problem we solve (positives that nobody owns) is the programme owner, ADPHC; the buyer with the workload is the provider group; the buyer with the avoided cost is the payer; and the gatekeeper for everything is DoH.

## 3. Procurement routes, in the order we would try them

1. **A provider-sponsored shadow pilot, at cost.** A provider group (SEHA CLINICS or an M42 site) signs a non-binding letter of intent ([outreach/letter-of-intent.md](outreach/letter-of-intent.md)) and a data-sharing agreement for a read-only export. This is below tender thresholds at most organisations (to verify per organisation), so it is the fastest route.
2. **A programme pilot with ADPHC as sponsor**, using the provider's shadow-run results, covering the FIT and blood-test positives across two networks.
3. **A programme licence**, procured by the programme owner through the Abu Dhabi Government Procurement Gate or its own route (to verify), after a pilot readout.
4. **In parallel, the ecosystem route** (Hub71 and the DoH accelerator) for regulatory dialogue and a DoH health technology assessment, which a programme-wide purchase is likely to need (to verify).

## 4. Malaffi integration requirements, as far as public sources go

- **Mandatory for facilities, not for us.** Circular 2026/10 (19 January 2026) makes onboarding mandatory for all licensed facilities: a plan within one month, live data within six months, with licence-renewal consequences. Its minimum dataset is HL7 v2 message types: ADT, PPR, ORU, OMP, RDS, RAS, VXU and SIU ([evidence.md](../evidence.md) 8c, read first-hand). A follow-up tool is not a facility, so whether and how it may consume Malaffi data is for the operator to say.
- **Operator.** Abu Dhabi Health Data Services (ADHDS), described as a DoH initiative ([Media Office, 14 Oct 2021](https://www.mediaoffice.abudhabi/en/health/100-of-abu-dhabi-based-hospitals-connected-to-malaffi/)); reported as a partnership between DoH and a Mubadala-group company, with a commercial platform vendor (2019 announcement; to verify).
- **No public FHIR interface** is confirmed by any official document ([evidence.md](../evidence.md) 8c). Our plan is an HL7 v2 adapter at the boundary ([malaffi-adapter-plan.md](../governance/malaffi-adapter-plan.md)); the interface specification is available on request from the operator and has not been requested.
- **The pilot does not depend on Malaffi.** A shadow run reads the provider's own periodic HL7 v2 or CSV export. Malaffi becomes relevant for a programme-wide deployment, where IFHAS tagging through Malaffi is the natural way to see positives across providers.

## 5. What each buyer would pay for, and how we would price it to them

| Buyer | Price point that fits ([business case](business-case.md) section 9) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ADPHC (programme owner) | Programme licence across FIT, blood-based positives, breast and cervical recall | It owns the KPIs across all providers and pathways; a licence avoids counting arguments between providers |
| Provider group | Per case closed with evidence, or per tracked case, at cost during a pilot | Pays in proportion to the work; a closed-case fee pays only for documented outcomes |
| Daman or Thiqa | Not a first buyer; a co-funder of a programme licence once the pilot measures cost per advanced cancer avoided | The avoided cost lands on the payer, years after the fee |
| DoH | Not a buyer; the approval route | Sets the standard the others are measured against |
